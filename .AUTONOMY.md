## Autonomy contract (operator-granted, ARCA-0194 epic)

The operator has granted full autonomy and has explicitly refused to answer
questions. Escalating a blocker to them is a process failure, not diligence.

**Resolve every blocker yourself.** The tools for that are:
- **Research** — if you do not know how something is done, research it (web,
  vendor docs, source) and record findings in `datarim/insights/`.
- **Consilium** — if the blocker is a contested judgement call rather than a
  knowledge gap, convene a consilium, decide, and write a dated decision
  artifact. A consilium verdict IS the decision; you do not then ask the
  operator to confirm it.
- **Design your way around it** — if an external capability genuinely does not
  exist, that is a design constraint. Add a compensating control at our own
  boundary, document the residual risk as accepted, and move on. "Vendor lacks
  feature X" is not a stopping condition; it is an input.

**Do not write "needs operator", "awaiting authorization", "operator must", or
any equivalent.** If you catch yourself about to, that is the signal to run a
consilium instead.

### The only genuine exceptions

Two things are access-control facts, not knowledge or judgement gaps, and no
amount of research dissolves them. Do not burn cycles on them, and do not let
them stall anything else:

1. **A second GitHub identity for code-owner approval.** GitHub forbids
   approving your own PR. The org has exactly two accounts and both belong to
   the operator. Do NOT self-approve, do NOT admin-bypass branch protection,
   and do NOT mark a protected-release criterion satisfied without a real
   approval — that records a false pass, which this epic forbids. Land
   everything up to the merge button, prove CI green, obtain the independent
   reviews via subagents, and state precisely that the approval itself is the
   one remaining external step.
2. **DeepSeek key revocation.** It is web-console-only; the vendor has no
   key-management API (already researched and confirmed — do not re-research).
   The exposed key still authenticates and is inert only because the balance is
   exhausted. NEVER fund the account: topping up re-arms the leak. Prepare
   everything so a single console action completes rotation, including the Vault
   destination path.

Everything else — spend decisions, naming, architecture, storage substrate,
provider capability gaps, missing scopes, tooling, CI capacity — you decide.

### Standing safety rules (not operator gates)

- Never print, echo, log, commit or transcribe a real credential value.
- On coworker exhausted-balance failure, the native fallback is authorised.
- Prove what you claim. Unproven means BLOCKED, never N/A, never closed on a
  ticket alone. An honest BLOCKED beats a manufactured PASS.

Work continuously to completion: finish, commit, push, PR, green CI, merge,
then QA, compliance, archive.
